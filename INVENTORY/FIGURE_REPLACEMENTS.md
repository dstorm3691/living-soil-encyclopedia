# Pulled Figures: Replace or Drop

Every figure pulled from v1.0, why it was pulled, and what a replacement has to show.
Mark a decision on each one. `pulled_figures.md` is the machine log; this file is the working list.

**Pull rule:** photoreal AI diagnostic imagery, production text printed in the image, or content that teaches something false. Typos alone do not pull a figure.

**Pulled so far:** 43
**Last updated:** 2026-09-29

---

## Decision key

| Option | Means |
|---|---|
| **REPLACE** | Find a real, licensed photo or diagram. Source leads given per figure. |
| **FIX IMAGE** | The figure is fine except for printed text. Edit the text out of the image file and restore the figure. Fastest option. |
| **REDRAW** | Concept is worth a visual, but the image is wrong. Needs a new illustrated (not photoreal) figure with corrected content. |
| **DROP** | The text carries the section, or another kept figure already covers it. Close the slot. |

### LSE_B03 Nematode Mouthpart Comparison
- **Section:** Nematode Diversity: Beyond the Root-Knot
- **Showed:** Four nematode heads (bacterial feeder, fungal feeder, predator, root feeder) with example genera, plus a root-knot damage panel.
- **Why pulled:** The four "electron micrographs" are AI fabrications with 10 µm scale bars. It also listed *Ditylenchus* and *Aphelenchoides* as "usually beneficial" fungal feeders; both genera include serious plant pests (stem and bulb nematode, foliar nematodes).

### LSE_A02 Soil Food Web Functional Map
- **Section:** Trophic Levels Defined
- **Showed:** Plants, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, microarthropods and earthworms with carbon, grazing and mineralization flows.
- **Why pulled:** Earthworms are placed as the top predator tier, with arrows showing them eating predatory nematodes and microarthropods. Earthworms are decomposers and shredders, not predators.
- **A replacement must show:** A standard soil food web: photosynthesizers, then bacteria and fungi, then shredders and grazers, then predators, with earthworms in the shredder and decomposer role.
- **Source leads:** The USDA NRCS Soil Biology Primer soil food web diagram is the classic version of this figure and is a US government publication, likely public domain. Confirm its credit line before use. This is the single most useful replacement on the list.
- **Decision:** [ ] Replace  [ ] Redraw  [ ] Drop

### UCI_003 The Nutrient Mineralization Loop
- **Section:** The Nutrient Loop: Predation and Mineralization
- **Showed:** Organic matter to ammonium to nitrite to nitrate, with immobilization and denitrification.
